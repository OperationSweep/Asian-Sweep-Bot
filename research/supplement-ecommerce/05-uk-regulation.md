# Phase 7 — UK Regulatory Requirements

> **Not legal advice.** This is commercial research. Before launch, commission a UK food-law consultant for a label and claims review (`[ESTIMATE]` £800–£2,500 for a 5-SKU range) and confirm everything below against current FSA/MHRA guidance.

---

## 7.1 The one distinction that matters most: food supplement vs medicinal product

This is where a supplement business gets destroyed, so it goes first.

| | **Food supplement** | **Medicinal product** |
|---|---|---|
| Governing law | Food Supplements (England) Regulations 2003 + equivalents in Scotland, Wales, NI; retained EU food law | Human Medicines Regulations 2012; MHRA |
| Pre-market approval | **None required** — but you must notify | **Full marketing authorisation required** |
| Cost to bring to market | Hundreds to low thousands of £ | Hundreds of thousands to millions |
| Permitted claims | Only those on the **GB Nutrition and Health Claims Register** | Claims per the authorised indication |

**`[FACT]`** MHRA determines status case-by-case under **Guidance Note 8, "A guide to what is a medicinal product"**, updated with revised borderline-products guidance published **17 October 2025**. Two independent limbs:

1. **By presentation** — is the product *presented as* preventing, treating or curing disease? This limb is triggered by **your marketing**, not your formulation.
2. **By function** — does it achieve its principal effect through a **pharmacological, immunological or metabolic mode of action**?

**`[ANALYSIS]` The commercially critical point: limb 1 means your TikTok ad can reclassify your product.** You can sell a perfectly legal magnesium capsule and, by running an ad that says "beats insomnia", cause that product to be treated as an unlicensed medicine — which is a criminal offence to place on the market, not merely an ASA problem. **The formulation is not the risk. The copy is the risk.**

### Practical firewall rules (adopt as company policy)

1. **No product name, strapline, ad, landing page, email, or reply-to-comment may reference a disease, condition or symptom by name.** Banned vocabulary: insomnia, anxiety, depression, ADHD, IBS, arthritis, diabetes, hypertension, menopause *as a condition being treated*, erectile dysfunction, low testosterone.
2. **Ban implied-treatment structures**: "the natural alternative to [drug]", "instead of sleeping pills", "doctors don't want you to know".
3. **Every claim traces to a register entry or is a plain product fact.** If it does neither, it does not ship.
4. **Comment moderation is a compliance control**, not a community-management nicety.
5. **If unsure, use the MHRA Medicines Borderline Advice Form** — free formal opinion, up to four products per submission `[FACT]`.

## 7.2 Regulator-by-regulator map

| Body | What they control | What we must do |
|---|---|---|
| **FSA (Food Standards Agency)** | Food law policy, novel foods, composition, additives | Ensure no ingredient requires **Novel Foods authorisation** (anything not consumed to a significant degree in the EU/UK before **1 May 1997**) `[FACT]`. Vet every ingredient against this before formulating |
| **Local Authority / Trading Standards** | Enforcement, food business registration | **Register the food business with the local council where we operate, at least 28 days before trading.** Free. Notify Trading Standards of supplement products placed on the market `[FACT]` |
| **MHRA** | Medicines borderline, POMs | Keep clear of medicinal presentation (7.1). Never sell melatonin/DHEA OTC |
| **ASA / CAP** | Advertising content, all media incl. influencer and organic social | CAP Code section 15 compliance. Free Copy Advice available |
| **HMRC** | VAT, duty | VAT registration and correct rating (7.7) |
| **OPSS / GPSR** | General product safety | Product safety obligations, traceability, recall capability |
| **ICO** | Data protection | GDPR/PECR for email and SMS marketing, cookie consent |

**`[FACT]`** There is **no FSA pre-approval to sell supplements in the UK.** The compliance burden is on you, continuously, and enforcement is reactive — which means the first time you hear about a problem is usually via an ASA ruling, a Trading Standards letter, or a platform ban.

## 7.3 Labelling — the mandatory checklist

`[FACT]` (Food Supplements Regs + FIC Regulation 1169/2011 as retained). A compliant GB label must carry:

- [ ] The words **"food supplement"**
- [ ] Product name
- [ ] Names of the nutrient categories or substances characterising the product
- [ ] **Recommended daily portion**
- [ ] **"Do not exceed the recommended daily dose"** warning
- [ ] **"Keep out of the reach of young children"**
- [ ] **"Food supplements should not be used as a substitute for a varied and balanced diet and a healthy lifestyle"**
- [ ] Amount of nutrients/substances **per recommended daily dose**, and **% NRV** where an NRV exists
- [ ] Full ingredient list in descending weight order, with **allergens emphasised** (bold/caps/underline) — the 14 regulated allergens
- [ ] Net quantity
- [ ] Best-before date and batch/lot code
- [ ] **Name and address of the UK food business operator** — post-Brexit this must be a **UK address**
- [ ] Storage conditions and instructions for use
- [ ] Country of origin where its omission would mislead

**`[ANALYSIS]` Three practical traps for a DTC brand:**
1. **The UK FBO address must be a real UK address on-pack.** A virtual office is generally acceptable; an overseas-only address is not.
2. **Your website is part of the label.** Distance-selling rules mean mandatory food information must be available **pre-purchase** on the product page — not only on the bottle. Most new supplement sites fail this.
3. **Allergen emphasis** — "may contain milk" hidden in body text is a common Trading Standards finding.

## 7.4 Ingredient and composition restrictions

`[FACT]`
- Only **permitted vitamin and mineral sources** listed in the Regulations may be used (this is why "magnesium glycinate" vs another salt matters legally, not just commercially).
- **Novel Foods**: pre-1997 consumption test. Requires FSA authorisation if not met.
- Certain botanicals carry restrictions or are effectively medicinal at dose (e.g. monacolin K in red yeast rice).
- **Maximum permitted levels**: GB has no single statutory upper-limit table for most nutrients, but **safe upper levels** (EVM/SACN guidance) apply in practice and Trading Standards act on excess. Vitamin D, vitamin A, selenium, iodine and iron are the common flashpoints.
- **Iodine in sea moss** is a live example — highly variable natural iodine content can exceed safe levels. This is a real reason sea moss is not in our range.

## 7.5 Manufacturing, GMP, traceability, recall

| Requirement | Detail | Our position |
|---|---|---|
| **GMP** | Not a statutory licence for food supplements in GB (unlike medicines), but it is the industry standard and the de facto requirement of every serious retailer, insurer and marketplace | **Non-negotiable supplier criterion** |
| **HACCP** | Legally required food safety management for food businesses | Manufacturer's responsibility; verify in audit |
| **ISO 22000 / BRCGS** | Voluntary but strongly preferred | Preferred supplier criterion |
| **Batch traceability** | Legally required — one step back, one step forward | Record batch → order mapping in Shopify from day one |
| **Certificate of Analysis (COA)** | Per batch: identity, potency, heavy metals, microbiology | **Demand for every batch. Publish on the website — it is also a marketing asset** |
| **Third-party testing** | Not mandatory; strongly recommended | Budget for it on hero SKUs |
| **Recall procedure** | Must be able to withdraw/recall and notify the FSA | Written procedure + retained batch samples before first sale |
| **Retained samples** | Industry practice: keep samples of each batch to shelf-life + 6 months | Adopt |

## 7.6 Import requirements

`[FACT/ANALYSIS]` If importing finished goods or ingredients into GB:
- The importer becomes the **UK food business operator responsible for compliance** — importing from a cheap overseas manufacturer transfers *all* legal risk onto you.
- Customs duty + **import VAT at 20%** (reclaimable if VAT-registered), EORI number required.
- Products of animal origin (**marine collagen, bovine collagen, colostrum, whey, fish oil**) attract additional import controls, health certification and border checks. **This materially affects three of our shortlisted products** — colostrum, marine collagen and any protein.
- **`[ANALYSIS]` Recommendation: manufacture in the UK for launch.** It removes import complexity, shortens lead times, supports a "Made in the UK" claim that genuinely converts, and keeps the compliance chain short. It costs more per unit and is worth it.

## 7.7 VAT

| Point | Detail | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Vitamin and mineral supplements in pill, capsule, powder or liquid form are **standard-rated at 20%** — they are not "food" for VAT purposes | `[FACT]` HMRC VFOOD guidance | `[FACT]` |
| Some powdered products can qualify for zero-rating in narrow circumstances (nutritional purpose rather than therapeutic) — genuinely contested, litigated, and fact-specific | `[FACT]` HMRC VFOOD1660 / FTT case law | `[FACT]` |
| **Do not build a business plan on winning a zero-rating argument.** Model everything at 20% | — | `[ANALYSIS]` |
| VAT registration threshold: £90,000 taxable turnover | `[FACT]` | `[FACT]` |

**`[ANALYSIS]` The VAT decision is more consequential than it looks.** Below the threshold you keep the 20%, which flatters early margins. The moment you register, **you lose 16.7% of every retail price overnight** unless you raise prices. Model the post-registration business from day one, and price for it. A plan that looks profitable at £80k revenue and breaks at £95k is a common and avoidable failure.

## 7.8 Insurance

| Cover | Why | Indicative cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Product liability** | Not legally required, **but required contractually** by Amazon, marketplaces, retailers and most 3PLs; also the only thing standing between you and a personal-injury claim | £1m–£5m limits typical; combined public + product liability **from ~£5–£15/month for micro-businesses**, realistically **£300–£900/yr** for a supplement brand at scale `[FACT: Simply Business/Hiscox published pricing; `[ESTIMATE]` for the supplement-specific figure] |
| **Product recall** | Recalls are the catastrophic-loss scenario; UK recall costs range from ~£450k to £5m+ depending on category `[FACT, cited industry research]` | Specialist cover, `[ESTIMATE]` £1,000–£3,000/yr |
| **Professional indemnity** | If giving any nutritional guidance | `[ESTIMATE]` £300–£600/yr |
| **Cyber** | Customer data + payment | `[ESTIMATE]` £300–£800/yr |

**`[ANALYSIS]` £5m product liability + recall cover from day one.** It is one of the cheapest risk reductions available and it is a gate to every serious retail or marketplace channel.
