# PHASE 18 — Honest failure analysis

## The three risks that actually kill this business

`[ANALYSIS]` Of the twenty-plus risks below, three are existential and seventeen are manageable. Founders reliably worry about the wrong ones.

### ☠️ Risk 1 — CAC never falls below contribution per order

**Probability: HIGH (~45%). Impact: TERMINAL.**

This is the base case failing to become the strong case. The Phase 15 analysis shows that at £55 CAC and £48 AOV, **no revenue tier is profitable** — the business loses money at £10k/month and at £250k/month. It is not a scale problem; scale amplifies it.

**Warning signs:** blended CAC flat or rising after month 4; bundle mix stuck under 30%; subscription attach under 10%.

**Mitigation:**
- **Bundle-first everything.** 70% bundle mix moves contribution per order by ~70% with no extra spend.
- **TikTok Shop affiliate/creator commission** converts fixed ad spend into variable cost of sale — it *cannot* produce a CAC you can't afford, because it's a revenue share.
- **Cap ad spend at ≤130% of trailing 30-day contribution.** Removes the scenario where you spend your way to zero.
- **Kill criteria at day 90** (`10-launch-and-content.md`), pre-committed in writing.

### ☠️ Risk 2 — Ad account / TikTok Shop ban

**Probability: MEDIUM-HIGH (~35% of at least one ban in 24 months). Impact: SEVERE to TERMINAL.**

`[FACT]` Health & wellness ads are flagged for manual review **1.6× more than any other category**, with **20,000+ flagged in H1 2025 alone**. TikTok Shop prohibits whole product classes outright. A single-channel business with one banned account is a dead business.

**Mitigation:**
- Two Business Managers, two ad accounts, two payment methods, two verified domains, from day one. **Set this up before you need it — you cannot build it while banned.**
- Compliance-first creative (Phase 6). Most bans are self-inflicted by claim escalation.
- **Diversify to owned channels early:** email and SMS lists are the only assets no platform can switch off. Target 25% of revenue from owned channels by month 12.
- Never route ad traffic through a domain you don't control.

### ☠️ Risk 3 — Cash exhaustion during the 12-month payback

**Probability: HIGH if under-capitalised (~50% at £5k–£10k). Impact: TERMINAL.**

`[DERIVED]` The base-case model shows **~£80,000 peak working-capital requirement** if run at full speed, with peak burn at month 14–16. Most founders start with £5k–£25k.

**Mitigation:** the self-funding ramp. Accept 18–24 months instead of 12. This is the single most important structural decision in the plan and it is made in the budget-tier analysis below.

# PHASE 20 — The investment decision

## 1. Is selling supplements via faceless social ecommerce commercially viable?

**Yes — but not in the form described in the brief, and not at the capital level most people assume.**

Three qualifications, each load-bearing:

- **"Faceless" must mean face-light, not trust-light.** `[FACT]` Supplements have the highest CAC of any DTC category (~$89) *and* the highest brand loyalty (71%). Anonymity is fine; anonymity plus no COAs, no reviews and no real company is not.
- **Single-product selling is arithmetically dead.** No single-unit order in the Top 10 covers even a best-case £38 CAC. **Bundles and subscription are not optimisations — they are the business model.**
- **It requires 18–24 months and a 12-month payback tolerance**, not a 90-day flip.

## 2. Would I personally pursue this business based on the evidence?

**Qualified yes — with a specific plan, and only under two conditions.**

`[ANALYSIS]` I would pursue it **if and only if**:
1. **You are building an asset, not an income.** £1m of revenue produces ~£58k of profit `[DERIVED]` — a poor income for the effort. But `[FACT]` Huel sold at ~3.9× revenue and Applied Nutrition listed at £107m. A high-retention subscription brand is worth far more sold than milked. **If the goal is monthly income, do something else.**
2. **You genuinely have, or can build, a content engine.** The entire thesis rests on producing 5–10 compliant short-form videos a day, indefinitely. If that will not happen, CAC never falls, and the Phase 15 tables show the business fails at every scale. **This is a media-production business that happens to sell powder.**

If either condition fails, I would not pursue it.

## 3. Strongest arguments FOR

1. **`[FACT]` 37.7% repurchase, 71% brand loyalty, $300–$1,200 subscription LTV** — the retention mechanics are genuinely excellent and rare.
2. **`[FACT]` Creatine at 26.2% CAGR with a broadening audience** — a real, large, fast-growing wave with an unoccupied position on it.
3. **`[FACT]` 65–85% gross margins** — enough headroom to absorb a high CAC if retention works.
4. **`[FACT]` Proven exits and precedents**: AG1 ~$600m on effectively one SKU; Huel £254m → ~€1bn; Applied Nutrition £0 → £107m and an LSE listing. **This is not a category where success is theoretical.**
5. **Low capital to *test*.** ~£9,400 of inventory validates the thesis with real customers in 90 days.
6. **`[FACT]` TikTok Shop is now the UK's 4th-largest beauty and wellness retailer** — a genuine distribution channel that did not exist five years ago, with commission-based economics that cap downside.
7. **Compliance is a moat.** Most TikTok-first competitors are non-compliant and structurally fragile.

## 4. Strongest arguments AGAINST

1. **`[DERIVED]` The base case breaks even at exactly 12 months and makes £6,500 of net profit in year two.** That is the *realistic* case, not the pessimistic one.
2. **`[FACT]` Supplements have the highest CAC of any DTC category.** You are entering the hardest acquisition environment in ecommerce.
3. **`[FACT]` The UK online supplement channel is growing at 1% a year (IBISWorld, £1.5bn).** There is no rising tide. Every pound must be taken from an incumbent.
4. **`[DERIVED]` ~£80,000 peak working capital** to run the plan at full speed — 3–8× what most founders budget.
5. **Existential platform dependency.** `[FACT]` 20,000+ health ads flagged in H1 2025; Meta's tiered system removes lookalike audiences for restricted health ads — the standard DTC scaling mechanism is unavailable to you.
6. **Zero product moat.** Your creatine is chemically identical to everyone else's, forever.
7. **`[FACT]` Huel makes 7.6% net margin at £254m.** This is a structurally low-net-margin industry, and no cleverness changes that.
8. **Regulatory tail risk is asymmetric.** One contaminated batch or one MHRA reclassification ends the business; there is no equivalent upside event.

## 5. What should we sell first?

**Three SKUs: Creatine Monohydrate 500g (Creapure®), Electrolytes 30-stick, Magnesium Glycinate 120 caps.** Sold primarily as the **Foundation Stack at £64.99** with a **Starter Bundle at £34.99** for cold traffic. ~£9,400 opening inventory.

## 6. What should we definitely avoid?

Weight loss, fat burners, appetite suppressants, detox/cleanse (**prohibited on TikTok Shop, restricted on Meta**); testosterone and sexual-performance positioning (**prohibited**); melatonin and DHEA (**POM in GB — illegal to sell OTC**); CBD and NMN (Novel Foods exposure); shilajit (heavy-metal contamination); berberine (medicinal-claim territory); sea moss (iodine variability); **whey protein at launch** (WPC +108%, WPI +139% `[FACT]`); generic multivitamins (own-label price floor); **dropshipping** (22/100 — arithmetically impossible at a £70 CAC); and **before/after creative of any kind**.

## 7. Dropship, wholesale, private-label or manufacture?

**UK private label (78/100) with a subscription layer (88/100), migrating to custom formulation in Year 2.**

- ❌ **Dropship (22/100)** — 20% margin against a £70 CAC. Not a marginal call; it is impossible.
- ❌ **Wholesale (41/100)** — no brand equity, no pricing power, competes with Amazon on identical SKUs.
- ✅ **Private label (78/100)** — 65–85% margin, £6k–£20k entry, 4–10 week lead time, full customer ownership.
- ⏳ **Custom formulation (66/100)** — the right destination, wrong starting point. £25k–£80k and 12–24 weeks is too large a bet on an untested market read. **Fund it from private-label profit once you know what sells.**
- ⭐ **Subscription (88/100)** — mandatory layer, not an alternative.

**`[ANALYSIS]` Recommended: £25,000, run on a self-funding ramp.** It is the smallest amount that lets you execute the real plan rather than a compromised version of it, and the self-funding rule converts the £80,000 peak-capital problem into a time problem instead of a solvency problem.

## 9. What could give us a genuine competitive advantage?

Ranked by defensibility `[ANALYSIS]`:

1. **A working, documented, compliant content engine.** Everything else is buyable; this compounds and cannot be bought. It is the actual asset.
2. **Radical batch transparency** — published per-batch COAs. ~£300/batch. Large brands cannot match this quickly because it exposes their variability.
3. **Owning the healthy-ageing / GLP-1 companion position** before an incumbent moves. A 12–24 month window at most.
4. **Range discipline as identity** — "five products, every dose from the research" is a positioning a 1,000-SKU competitor structurally cannot copy.
5. **Subscription with visibly honest cancellation** — differentiator *and* merchant-account protection.
6. **Owned channel depth** (email/SMS) — the only distribution no platform can revoke.
7. **A named, real, contactable UK operation** — in a trust category full of anonymous stores, this converts.

**Not advantages:** the formula (identical to everyone's), the manufacturer (shared with dozens of brands), price (you lose to vertically integrated competitors), packaging design (copied in a fortnight).

# ⚖️ VERDICT: **MODIFY — then GO**

**Not GO**, because the model as briefed — faceless social, 50 products, single-product selling — fails on the arithmetic in Phases 10 and 15. It would lose money at every revenue tier.

**Not NO-GO**, because the underlying category mechanics are genuinely strong (37.7% repurchase, 71% loyalty, 65–85% gross margins, a 26% CAGR hero product, and three proven UK/US exits), and the required modifications are all within the operator's control.

## The modifications required, in order of importance

1. **Bundle-first, always.** Foundation Stack at £64.99 is the default offer. Single units are the downgrade path. This alone is the difference between −£40 and −£21 on a first order.
2. **Subscription-first, not subscription-optional.** The 12-month payback means one-off buyers are unprofitable customers. Target 22%+ attach at launch, 32% by month 12.
3. **Three SKUs, not fifty.** Range discipline is the positioning.
4. **Face-light, not trust-light.** Real UK company, published COAs, aggressive review accumulation, named humans. Faceless creative is fine; faceless *business* is not.
5. **Self-funding ramp, mandatory.** Ad spend capped at ≤130% of trailing 30-day contribution. Converts an £80,000 solvency risk into an 18–24 month timeline.
6. **Compliance as strategy, not overhead.** Register-authorised claims only, blacklisted vocabulary, comment moderation, CAP Copy Advice, duplicate ad infrastructure from day one.
7. **Own the healthy-ageing / creatine-for-everyone position** rather than fighting for 18–34 gym men on TikTok. It is the least contested and highest-LTV audience available.
8. **Build for an exit, not for income.** £1m of revenue is worth ~£58k of profit and ~£2–4m as an asset. Optimise retention, brand and subscription base accordingly.

**Recommended commitment: £25,000, 3 SKUs, 90-day pre-committed kill criteria, self-funding ramp, decision point at day 90 and again at month 9.**

**If the day-90 criteria are not met, stop.** The largest risk in this business is not losing £25,000; it is spending three years and £80,000 discovering that a £75 CAC never came down.
