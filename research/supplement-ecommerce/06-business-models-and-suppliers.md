## Model-by-model verdict `[ANALYSIS]`

### Model A — Dropshipping established supplement brands: **22/100. Do not do this.**

This is the model most commonly paired with "faceless TikTok ads", and for supplements it is close to arithmetically impossible.

**The killer maths `[DERIVED]`:** dropship gross margin on a branded supplement is realistically 15–25%. On a £30 order at 20% that is **£6.00 gross profit**. Supplement CAC benchmarks are **~$89 (≈£70)** `[FACT]`. You need **12 repeat orders to recover one acquisition** — and you have no ability to drive repeat purchase because the customer bought *Optimum Nutrition*, not you. They will reorder on Amazon next time, at a lower price, with next-day delivery.

Add: you cannot control quality or batch documentation, you carry distributor liability without manufacturer control, delivery times destroy reviews, and you are competing on price against Amazon on products where the price is a Google search away. **No amount of good marketing fixes a 20% margin in a £70-CAC category.**

### Model B — Wholesale/reselling: **41/100.** A real business, but the wrong one for us.

30–40% margin is workable in *retail* (footfall, basket-building, no CAC per item) but not in paid-social DTC. It requires working capital, gives no brand equity, and pits you against Amazon and H&B on identical SKUs. Viable as a **supplementary** revenue line — stocking two or three trusted third-party brands alongside your own range to look like a legitimate store and lift AOV. Not viable as the core.

### Model C — UK private label: **78/100. This is the recommendation.**

You take an existing, proven manufacturer formulation, put your brand, dose and packaging on it, and own the customer relationship.

- **Margin** is 65–85% — enough to absorb a £50–£70 CAC and still build LTV.
- **Capital** is £6k–£20k for a 3–5 SKU launch, which is inside every budget tier we're considering.
- **Speed to market** is 4–10 weeks, so you learn from real customers within a quarter.
- **Regulatory burden is real but manageable** (~£5k/yr, per `05-uk-regulation.md`) and is itself a moat against sloppier competitors.
- **The weakness is defensibility:** your creatine is chemically identical to everyone else's. **Defensibility must therefore come from brand, content, community, service and range architecture — not from the molecule.** This is the central strategic challenge of the business and is addressed in `08-competitors-and-positioning.md`.

### Model D — Custom formulation: **66/100. Right destination, wrong starting point.**

Genuinely differentiated formulas, defensible IP, and the ability to build something acquirable — Applied Nutrition (£107m revenue, 46.7% GM `[FACT]`) and Huel (£254m, sold for ~€1bn `[FACT]`) are both fundamentally formulation businesses. But **£25k–£80k, 12–24 week lead times and 3,000–10,000 unit MOQs** mean you are betting a large sum on an untested market read.

**`[ANALYSIS]` The correct sequence is C → D.** Private-label to find out what actually sells and who actually buys it, then invest the profit in custom formulation for the 1–2 SKUs that have proven demand. Skipping to D is how founders lose £50k on 8,000 units of a product nobody wanted.

### Model E — Subscription: **88/100. Not an alternative model — a mandatory layer.**

Given first-order economics that lose ~£30–£50 per customer, **subscription is not an optimisation, it is the business model.** The supporting evidence is strong: supplements have a **37.7% repurchase rate** `[FACT]`, subscription LTV of **$300–$600 for vitamins and $600–$1,200 for premium greens** `[FACT]`, and **71% brand loyalty** once established `[FACT]`.

**Recommendation: Model C + E — UK private label sold subscription-first — migrating selected SKUs to Model D (custom formulation) in Year 2.**

# PHASE 9 — Suppliers and manufacturing

## Selection criteria (in priority order)

`[ANALYSIS]` Ranked by how much each reduces the probability of catastrophic failure, not by cost:

1. **GMP certification, verifiable** — ask for the certificate and the certifying body, then check it
2. **Per-batch COA including heavy metals and microbiology** — non-negotiable
3. **Third-party (not in-house) lab verification available** on hero SKUs
4. **UK-based manufacture** — removes import controls, shortens lead time, supports a Made-in-UK claim
5. **MOQ ≤ 1,000 units** at launch
6. **Allergen segregation and cross-contamination controls** — legal exposure
7. **Batch traceability and retained samples**
8. **Custom packaging capability** (pouches, tubs, bottles, and full artwork support)
9. **Formulation support** for the Model D transition later
10. **Fulfilment / white-label dispatch**, ideally Shopify-integrated
11. **BRCGS / ISO 22000 / Informed Sport** where relevant
12. Price — **last**, deliberately

## The RFQ — what to send, and the questions that separate good from bad

Send an identical brief to **five** UK manufacturers. Vary nothing. Then compare.

**Brief contents:** 5 SKUs (creatine monohydrate 500g pouch; electrolyte sticks 30ct box; magnesium glycinate 120 caps; creatine gummies 60ct; greens powder 30 servings), quantities at 500 / 1,000 / 2,500 / 5,000 units, packaging format, target retail price, launch date.

**Ask for, in writing:**
1. Copy of the current **GMP certificate** and the certifying body
2. A **specimen COA** for a comparable recent batch — check it actually includes heavy metals (lead, arsenic, cadmium, mercury) and microbiology, not just potency
3. **Ingredient origin and grade** — is the creatine Creapure® or generic Chinese monohydrate? Is the ashwagandha KSM-66 or unstandardised root powder? *This single question exposes half the market.*
4. **Stability data / shelf-life justification** — and for gummies specifically, **assayed creatine content at 0, 6 and 12 months**
5. Allergen controls and cross-contamination policy
6. Lead time at each volume, and payment terms (expect 50% deposit / 50% pre-dispatch on a first order)
7. Whether they hold **product liability insurance** and its limit
8. Whether they will supply artwork templates and a compliance check of your label
9. Whether they offer fulfilment/dispatch and whether it integrates with Shopify
10. **Who else do you make this exact product for?** — tells you whether you are buying a differentiated formula or the same white-label SKU as fifteen TikTok brands

**Red flags `[ANALYSIS]`:** unwillingness to share GMP certification; COAs without heavy-metal panels; "we can make any claim you want on the label"; no UK address; pressure to buy 10,000 units on a first order; conspicuously cheap gummy pricing (usually means under-dosed actives).

## Fulfilment options

| Option | Cost `[ESTIMATE]` | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-fulfil from home/unit** | £0 labour cost (your time), postage only | 0–500 orders/month. **Start here** — it teaches you the product, the packaging failures and the customer |
| **UK 3PL** | £2.20–£3.20 pick/pack + storage + postage | 500+ orders/month |
| **Manufacturer-integrated fulfilment** | Bundled | Convenient, but concentrates supplier risk. Avoid single-point dependency |
| **Amazon FBA** | Referral ~15% + FBA fees | Channel-specific; see `08-competitors-and-positioning.md` |

**Postage `[ESTIMATE]`:** Royal Mail Tracked 48 large letter £2.10–£2.90; small parcel £3.20–£4.20. **Designing packaging to fit the large-letter format is worth ~£1.20 per order — roughly 4–6% of net revenue.** For a business with a 30% contribution margin, that is a fifth of your profit sitting in a cardboard-thickness decision. It is worth engineering deliberately.
