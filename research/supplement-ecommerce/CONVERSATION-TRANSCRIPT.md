What survives the stress test:

- **Creatine is the standout product opportunity globally** — 26.2% CAGR (fastest of anything researched), ~£3.20 COGS at £29.99, an authorised GB health claim, and an audience broadening to women and over-45s that no incumbent addresses. - **Launch three SKUs, not five, not fifty** — creatine, electrolytes, magnesium glycinate. ~£9,400 inventory.
- **£25,000 on a self-funding ramp.** Full-speed peak working capital models at ~£80k; capping ad spend at ≤130% of trailing contribution turns that solvency risk into an 18–24 month timeline.

Three things you should push back on me about if you disagree, because they drive everything downstream:

## First, the honest criticism of my own work

The most load-bearing number in 30,000 words came from a Tier 3 benchmark aggregator selling a CRO service. So I rebuilt it from channel primitives instead: `CAC = (CPM ÷ 1000) ÷ CTR ÷ CVR`.

| Channel | Bear | Base | Bull |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meta (UK CPM £7.50–£14) | £183 | **£66** | £23 |
| TikTok paid | £131 | **£41** | £13 |

Blended base lands at **£50–£60**. Two independent methods agreeing to within 15% is what gives the number credibility — not any single source.

I also resolved a conflict that looked damaging: Triple Whale reports Meta cost-per-purchase for supplements at **$45.62**, roughly half the $89 CAC I used. They're not contradictory — one counts all platform-attributed purchases, the other counts new customers only. The 1.95× ratio sits exactly where platform-CPA-to-blended-CAC normally sits. **That corroborates the range rather than breaking it.**

## What I got wrong

**1. The trajectory was wrong-signed.** I assumed CAC falls £80 → £52 over 12 months. But Health & Wellness had the **sharpest CPA increase of any vertical, +12.6% YoY**, as supplement brands flood the platform. Category inflation partly cancels learning effects. Revised to **£80 → £68**, which pushes base-case Year 1 EBITDA from −£12k to about −£16.5k.

**2. 🚨 The plan can't exit Meta's learning phase.** This is a genuine arithmetic contradiction I missed. Meta needs ~50 conversions per ad set per week. At £55 CAC that's **£11,930/month per ad set** — my model doesn't reach that until month 12. So I assumed CAC would fall 35% during exactly the eleven months the account can't stabilise. Those assumptions can't both be true.

Fix: optimise for add-to-cart early (~£1,950/month clears the threshold instead of £11,930), consolidate to one broad ad set, and don't rely on Meta purchase campaigns before month 7–9. **This kills the £5k budget tier's Meta strategy entirely.**

**3. No seasonality.** UK CPMs run **+35–60% Sept–Dec** and **−25–40% in January**. That's a ±45% CAC swing I modelled as flat. Launch in January, and seasonally adjust the day-90 criteria — otherwise a Q4 launch produces a false negative and you kill a viable business.

## The finding that changes the plan

**TikTok Shop affiliate is first-order profitable where Meta is not**, because commission is a variable cost of sale — you cannot overspend it into nothing.

| Foundation Stack £64.99 | Meta | TikTok Shop |
|---|---|---|
| First-order contribution | **−£21.56** | **+£16.36** |

Even on a bare £29.99 creatine tub at a 30% creator commission it stays positive (+£3.52). TikTok's own 9% platform fee is Tier 1 published data.

I flagged TikTok Shop as a *mitigation* and then built the whole financial model on paid-social CAC. That was a mistake — it should be the primary channel for months 1–6, with Meta funded from its cash later. The real caveat: TikTok partly owns the customer, which attacks the LTV thesis the business depends on.

## The number you should actually target

| Blended CAC | 24-mo LTV:CAC | |
|---|---|---|
| £34 | 3.01 | **The target** |
| £55 | 1.86 | Base — viable, thin |
| £68 | 1.50 | Revised base — marginal |
| £102 | 1.00 | Hard stop |

And the reframe that matters most: **CAC ÷ retention is the constraint, not CAC.** At 24-month retention the business tolerates £100; at 12-month retention it tolerates £55. Doubling retention is worth exactly as much as halving CAC — and it's cheaper to do. My original research treated CAC as the villain when the ratio is the real variable.

**Verdict unchanged: MODIFY → GO.** Slightly worse base case, slightly more capital, slightly longer timeline — but a channel with better first-order economics than anything in the original plan.

The thing this exercise really proved isn't a number. It's that CAC has a plausible **14× range** across scenarios and no further desk research narrows it. The first 90 days of real spend is the only instrument that measures it — which makes the pre-committed kill criteria the most valuable single thing in the research.
