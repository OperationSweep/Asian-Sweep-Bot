# Phase 4 — Ranking: Top 20 → Top 10 → Top 5

## Scoring methodology

`[ANALYSIS]` The Phase 4 brief listed 21 factors. Treating them equally would be wrong — several are near-worthless discriminators for a UK startup (every supplement has 24-month shelf life; nearly all are private-labellable). I weighted them by how much each actually moves the probability of *this* business succeeding at *this* capital level.

| Weight | Factor | Why this weight |
|---|---|---|
| **15** | Absolute gross profit per order (£, not %) | At £50–£70 CAC this is the binding constraint. Percentage margin is a vanity metric |
| **12** | Repeat purchase + subscription potential | The only route to profit, given first order loses money |
| **12** | Social/visual demonstrability (TikTok + Reels) | The chosen acquisition channel. If it can't be shown, it can't be sold this way |
| **10** | Category growth rate | Riding a 26% CAGR is worth more than out-executing on a 1% one |
| **10** | Regulatory/claims headroom | A product you can't legally describe can't be marketed |
| **8** | Competition intensity & Amazon price floor | Determines whether your price holds |
| **8** | Brand differentiation opportunity | Determines whether you build equity or rent traffic |
| **7** | Bundle/upsell/cross-sell fit | The AOV lever that fixes the CAC problem |
| **6** | Shipping cost & format (letterbox-friendly?) | £2.90 vs £4.20 per order is 4–5% of margin |
| **5** | Supplier availability & MOQ at low capital | Determines whether you can start at all |
| **4** | Input-cost volatility | Whey exposure is a live, current, margin-destroying risk |
| **3** | Shelf life | Near-universal 24 months; weak discriminator |
| **100** | | |

## TOP 5 TO LAUNCH FIRST

These five are **not five independent products. They are one coherent system.** That is deliberate and it is the core recommendation of this research.

### 1. Creatine Monohydrate (Creapure®-grade, 250g/500g) — **the hero**

- **Economics `[ESTIMATE]`:** COGS £3.20 (500g) → retail £29.99 inc VAT → net £24.99 → **GP £21.79, 87% GM**
- **Why it beats the alternatives:** it is the only product in the database where the growth rate (26.2% CAGR `[FACT]`), the margin, the content potential and the regulatory headroom are *all* top-quartile. Whey has the demand but the margin is being destroyed by a documented 108–139% input-cost surge `[FACT]`. Collagen has the margin but a 6.8% CAGR. Lion's Mane has the content but the claims risk. Creatine has no equivalent weakness.
- **The asymmetric advantage:** the audience is broadening from "gym men 18–34" to **women, over-45s, and cognitive/healthy-ageing users** `[FACT, 2026 trend reporting]`. The incumbents' brand equity is all built on the *old* audience. **A creatine brand built for the new audience is a genuine positioning gap, not a me-too.**
- **Claims headroom:** an authorised GB claim exists for creatine and physical performance in short-term high-intensity exercise. That is a legal, on-register claim — a rarity in this category.

### 2. Electrolytes (30 stick packs, 2–3 flavours) — **the subscription engine**

- **Economics `[ESTIMATE]`:** COGS £4.50 → retail £26.99 → net £22.49 → **GP £17.99, 80% GM**
- **Why it beats the alternatives:** 30-day replenishment is the shortest cycle of any non-protein product. Subscription attach rate should be the highest in the range. Flavours give a legitimate reason to return that vitamins never have. Ships as a large letter.
- **Strategic role:** this is the product that converts a one-off buyer into an LTV. Without it, the CAC maths in `07-unit-economics.md` do not close.

### 3. Magnesium Glycinate (120 caps) — **the mass-market entry point**

- **Economics `[ESTIMATE]`:** COGS £2.80 → retail £22.99 → net £19.16 → **GP £16.36, 85% GM**
- **Why it beats the alternatives:** the biggest addressable audience of the five, the cheapest CPMs (the "can't sleep / always tired" hook works on every platform), and — critically — **authorised on-register claims exist** ("magnesium contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue", "…to normal psychological function", "…to normal muscle function") `[FACT, GB NHC Register]`. We can advertise it aggressively and legally.

### 4. Creatine Gummies (60ct) — **the TikTok weapon**

- **Economics `[ESTIMATE]`:** COGS £5.50 → retail £27.99 → net £23.32 → **GP £17.82, 76% GM**
- **Why it beats the alternatives:** TikTok Shop listings of creatine gummies have exceeded a million units `[FACT]`. Gummies are inherently demonstrable on video in a way powder is not. **Most importantly: gummies break the price comparison.** A shopper can compare £/kg on creatine powder in three seconds; they cannot easily compare gummies. That is where margin is defended.
- **Caveat `[ANALYSIS]`:** creatine stability in a gummy matrix is a real formulation issue. **Demand third-party assay of creatine content at 0/6/12 months from any manufacturer.** Several US brands have been caught under-delivering. This is a supplier-selection gate, not a reason to skip the product.

### 5. Greens Powder (30 servings) — **the AOV anchor**

- **Economics `[ESTIMATE]`:** COGS £8.50 → retail £44.99 → net £37.49 → **GP £28.99, 77% GM**
- **Why it beats the alternatives:** it is the only product in the top 5 that delivers **£29 gross profit on a single unit**. At a £55 CAC, one greens subscription is contribution-positive by month two; a £13-GP vitamin bottle never is. AG1 has proved the ceiling (~USD 600m on one SKU `[FACT]`) *and* proved the price point can hold at £79 — we can undercut hard at £44.99 and still make more absolute profit than on any other SKU.
- **Caveat:** greens is the most crowded of the five and the most formulation-dependent. Launch it third, not first (see `10-launch-and-content.md`).

## Why these five beat the alternatives — the systemic argument

`[ANALYSIS]` Judged individually, several rejected products (whey, collagen, probiotics) would score respectably. The five above win because of what they do **together**:

1. **They share one customer.** A 34-year-old who trains 3× a week and sleeps badly buys creatine, electrolytes and magnesium. That is a **cross-sell rate near 100%**, which is the only reliable way to lift AOV from £28 to £55 — and AOV is the variable that decides whether a £60 CAC is survivable.
2. **They span both cycle lengths.** Electrolytes and greens burn in 30 days; creatine and magnesium in 60–90. That produces a **staggered replenishment calendar** — a reason to email the customer every month without being annoying.
3. **They are all letterbox- or small-parcel-friendly.** No 1kg protein tubs. UK fulfilment cost stays at ~£5.00–£6.50 all-in rather than £8+.
4. **Not one of them requires a claim we cannot legally make.** No weight loss, no testosterone, no sleep-aid medicinal framing, no cognition claim, no disease reference. **Every one can be advertised on Meta and TikTok as written.** Given that the entire business model rests on paid social, this is not a footnote — it is the deciding criterion.
5. **Only one (greens) has meaningful input-cost volatility, and none has whey exposure.** In a market where Glanbia's segment EBITDA margin fell 380bps on whey inflation `[FACT]`, that is a deliberate and material structural advantage.

**The five-product range is the product.** Selling them as a range with bundles and a subscription is the business; selling them as five listings is not.
