# PHASE 5 — Is faceless social genuinely viable for supplements?

## The honest headline answer

**`[ANALYSIS]` Partially. "Faceless" works for *content* and for *paid creative*. It does not work for *trust*, and supplements are a trust category.**

Two evidence points define the boundary:

- **`[FACT]`** 55% of TikTok users report purchasing after seeing a product in a video, and TikTok Shop is now the UK's **4th-largest beauty and wellness retailer**. The channel works.
- **`[FACT]`** CRN: **71% of supplement users are loyal to their chosen brand**, and supplements carry the **highest CAC of any DTC category (~$89)**. Trust is the bottleneck, and trust is expensive to buy.

The reconciliation: **faceless content acquires attention cheaply; a credible brand converts it.** A faceless account with no named founder, no visible team, no lab documentation and no reviews will generate views and fail to convert at a rate that makes CAC work. The strategy is therefore not "faceless" but **"founder-optional, face-light, evidence-heavy"**.

## What "faceless" can and cannot do

| Faceless CAN | Faceless CANNOT |
|---|---|
| Generate top-of-funnel views at very low cost per 1,000 | Build the trust needed to justify £30 of a stranger's money on something they will ingest |
| Produce 5–10 creative variants/day for paid testing — the real advantage | Survive a "who are you?" comment thread with no answer |
| Run product demos, text-over-video, ingredient explainers, animated mechanism-of-action | Replace third-party reviews, COAs, and a real registered company |
| Scale across 3 platforms without talent costs or influencer negotiation | Sustain an organic account past ~50k followers without a personality |

**`[ANALYSIS]` Practical resolution:** run faceless as the **paid creative engine** and the **organic top-of-funnel**, and invest the saved talent budget into the three things that actually convert supplement buyers: **(1)** third-party lab results published on-site, **(2)** a fast-accumulating verified review base, **(3)** a real, named, contactable UK company. Hands, product, kitchen counters, gym floors, water bottles — no face needed. Voice, ideally a consistent one, is needed.

## Format-by-format assessment (July 2026)

| Format | Works? | Saturation | Notes `[ANALYSIS]` |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UGC-style faceless (hands + product + POV)** | **Yes — best performing** | High but still works | The workhorse. Cheap, native, endlessly variable |
| **Product demonstration (scoop, mix, dissolve, pour)** | **Yes** | Medium | Electrolytes and creatine are ideal; dissolution shots are genuinely satisfying |
| **Ingredient explainer** | **Yes** | Medium | Best trust-per-second of any format. Also the highest compliance risk — script these carefully |
| **Animated / motion-graphic mechanism** | Yes | **Low** | Underused, differentiating, expensive per unit but reusable |
| **Problem/solution** | Yes | **Very high** | Still converts on paid; organic reach is fatiguing badly |
| **Lifestyle / aesthetic b-roll** | Weak alone | High | Brand-building, poor direct response |
| **Gym footage** | Yes | **Extremely high** | Commoditised. Only works with a genuinely novel hook |
| **POV ("POV: you finally fixed your 3pm crash")** | Yes | High | Cheap to produce, strong hook rates |
| **Text-over-video** | **Yes** | High | Highest volume-per-hour format. Backbone of the 3–10/day engine |
| **AI-generated creative (voice, avatar, b-roll)** | **Yes, with caveats** | Rising fast | See warning below |
| **Educational / "3 things you didn't know"** | **Yes** | Medium | Excellent for creatine specifically — the audience is genuinely under-informed |
| **Before/after** | **NO** | — | **Prohibited by Meta for weight/body. Do not build any creative on this.** See Phase 6 |
| **Testimonial** | Legally constrained | Medium | Permitted **only** if it makes no health claim beyond the authorised register. "I remember to take it" is fine; "it cured my brain fog" is not |
| **Product comparison** | Yes | **Low** | Underused and effective — "creatine monohydrate vs HCl", "£/serving breakdown". Comparative claims must be factual and substantiable |
| **Morning/evening routine** | **Yes** | High | Perfect stack-selling vehicle. Naturally shows 3 products = AOV lift |

### The AI-creative warning `[ANALYSIS]`

AI voiceover and AI b-roll cut production cost by an order of magnitude and are now standard. Two consequences:
1. **The cost advantage is gone** because everyone has it. Volume alone is no longer an edge — hook quality is.
2. **Audience detection is rising.** Obviously-AI voice in a *trust* category is actively counterproductive. Use AI for scripting, editing, captions, b-roll and A/B variants; use a **real recorded human voice** for anything making a product claim. This is the single highest-ROI exception to "faceless".

## Platform-by-platform

| Platform | Role | Realistic expectation `[ESTIMATE]` |
|---|---|---|
| **TikTok organic** | Discovery + creative testing lab | 1 in 20–30 videos gets meaningful reach. Free, but not a reliable revenue channel. Its real value is telling you which hooks to put paid budget behind |
| **TikTok Ads** | Scaling channel for creatine/gummies/electrolytes | Cheap CPMs, young skew, **high ad-rejection rate in this vertical**. Budget for 15–25% creative rejection |
| **TikTok Shop** | **Probably the highest-ROI channel for this range** | 4th-largest UK beauty/wellness retailer `[FACT]`; 4.5–5.8% conversion in Health & Wellness `[ESTIMATE]`. Affiliate/creator commission model transfers CAC risk to a revenue share. **Do not ignore this in favour of a pure Shopify strategy** |
| **Instagram Reels (organic)** | Brand credibility + collagen/beauty/female audience | Slower growth than TikTok, better buyer quality |
| **Instagram Ads** | Retargeting + female-skewed prospecting | Higher CPMs, higher AOV, better subscription conversion |
| **Facebook Ads** | **The profit engine for 35+ audiences** | Highest CPMs but the highest-converting, highest-LTV audience. Under-used by supplement startups chasing TikTok. **For magnesium and healthy-ageing creatine this is the best channel, not TikTok** |
| **YouTube Shorts** | Long-tail, low priority at launch | Weak direct response; useful as a content repository and for SEO |

**`[ANALYSIS]` Channel priority for our Top 5:** TikTok Shop + TikTok Ads for creatine/gummies/electrolytes (young, gym, female-fitness) → Meta for magnesium and healthy-ageing creatine (35–65) → Instagram for greens and any future collagen → YouTube Shorts last.

# PHASE 6 — Advertising restrictions (the section that decides what you can actually say)

## The two-layer rule

`[ANALYSIS]` UK supplement marketing is governed by **two independent layers**, and you must satisfy both:

1. **The law + ASA/CAP** — what you may say *at all*, anywhere, in the UK.
2. **Platform policy** — what Meta/TikTok/Google will *accept*, which in several places is **stricter than UK law**.

Failing layer 1 gets you an ASA ruling and potentially MHRA/Trading Standards attention. Failing layer 2 gets your ad account banned — which for this business model is an extinction-level event. **Design creative to the stricter of the two, always.**

## Layer 1 — UK law and CAP Code (see also `05-uk-regulation.md`)

| Rule | Detail | Label |
|---|---|---|
| **Only authorised claims** | Only health/nutrition claims on the **GB Nutrition and Health Claims Register** may be used in marketing communications for food or food supplements | `[FACT, CAP Code s.15]` |
| **No disease claims — ever** | Claims that a food/supplement prevents, treats or cures human disease are **not permitted under any circumstances** | `[FACT, CAP Code]` |
| **Reduction-of-disease-risk claims** | Permitted only if on the register, and only presented without exaggeration | `[FACT]` |
| **Implied claims count** | Direct *or implied* medicinal claims are caught. Imagery, testimonials, hashtags and comments can all constitute a claim | `[FACT/ANALYSIS]` |
| **Active enforcement is real** | In **August 2025 alone the ASA published five rulings against food supplement ads** making unlicensed medicinal and unauthorised health claims. The ASA has also run an active 2025 line on CBD claims and influencer transparency | `[FACT]` |
| **Nutrient thresholds** | A claim may only be used where the product contains ≥15% of the NRV per 100g/portion | `[FACT]` |

## Layer 2 — Platform policy, by restricted topic

| Claim area | Meta | TikTok | Google/YouTube | Our position |
|---|---|---|---|---|

### The Meta targeting change is a bigger deal than the claims rules `[ANALYSIS]`

Meta's tiered health-ad system removing **custom and lookalike audiences** for restricted health ads is, commercially, more damaging than any wording restriction. Lookalikes off a purchaser list are the standard mechanism for scaling a DTC brand profitably. Without them:

- Prospecting must run **broad**, relying on Meta's algorithm and on creative to do the targeting.
- **Creative volume becomes the primary lever** — which is precisely the argument *for* the faceless content engine (Phase 17).
- **Retargeting warm audiences may also be restricted** depending on classification, which raises the value of **owned channels: email, SMS and subscription**.
- **Practical mitigation:** classify and word the product as a *food supplement with an authorised nutrition claim*, not a health-outcome product, to stay in the least-restricted tier where possible. Test this at account setup, not at scale-up.

## How to market aggressively **and** legally — the operating playbook

`[ANALYSIS]` Aggression should come from **hook, format, offer and volume**, never from claim escalation. Concretely:

**1. Shift aggression from the claim to the hook.**
- ❌ "Cures brain fog" → account risk + ASA ruling
- ✅ "I took creatine every day for 90 days. Here's what I actually tracked." → same attention, zero claim

**2. Use the register as a creative asset, not a constraint.** Authorised wording is legally bulletproof and can be stated boldly and repeatedly:
- "Magnesium contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue" `[FACT — authorised]`
- "Magnesium contributes to normal psychological function" `[FACT — authorised]`
- "Vitamin D contributes to normal immune system function" `[FACT — authorised]`
- Creatine + physical performance in short-term high-intensity exercise `[FACT — authorised]`

**3. Compete on verifiable product facts, not physiological promises.** These are aggressive, differentiating, and carry no claims risk whatsoever:
- "Creapure®. Third-party tested. Batch COA on every product page."
- "£0.29 per serving. Theirs is £1.10."
- "Five ingredients. All of them at the dose used in the research."
- "No proprietary blends."

**4. Sell the *behaviour*, not the *outcome*.** Routine content, habit-stacking, "what's in my gym bag", "my 6am stack" — all high-converting, all claim-free.

**5. Moderate your comments.** A user comment saying "this cured my insomnia" that you *like* or *reply to affirmatively* can be treated as a claim you have adopted. Set up comment moderation on day one and never affirm a health outcome.

**6. Pre-clear the risky 10%.** CAP offers a free Copy Advice service. Use it for anything near a line. It is free, it is fast, and it is documentary evidence of good faith.

**7. Assume account loss.** Run **two Business Managers, two ad accounts, two payment methods, verified domains, and a separate TikTok Shop entity**. This is not evasion — it is business continuity for a model whose single greatest operational risk is a platform ban (see `11-risks.md`).
