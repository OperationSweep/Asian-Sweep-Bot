# PHASE 16 — Phased product launch

## MVP — Launch with 3 SKUs, not 5

`[ANALYSIS]` The Phase 4 shortlist is five products; the *launch* should be **three**. Reason: each additional SKU costs £2,000–£4,000 of inventory, a separate label compliance review, separate COAs, separate creative, and dilutes the brand story before it has been tested. Three SKUs still supports the hero bundle, which is what matters.

| SKU | Format | Opening order | Cost `[ESTIMATE]` | Why it's in the MVP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Creatine Monohydrate 500g** (Creapure®) | Pouch, letterbox | 1,000 units | **£3,200** | The hero. 26% category CAGR, best margin, best claim headroom, the reason the brand exists |
| **2. Electrolytes, 30 sticks** (2 flavours) | Box, letterbox | 750 units | **£3,400** | The subscription engine. 30-day burn rate is what makes LTV work |
| **3. Magnesium Glycinate 120 caps** | Bottle, letterbox | 1,000 units | **£2,800** | The volume entry point. Cheapest CPMs, largest audience, register-authorised claims |
| | | **Total** | **~£9,400** | |

**The two offers that carry the MVP:**
- **Foundation Stack** (all three) — £64.99, £43.66 gross profit, **the default page**
- **Starter Bundle** (creatine 250g + electrolytes 15 sticks) — £34.99, £24.56 GP, the low-friction cold-traffic entry

**Do NOT launch with greens or gummies.** Greens is the most crowded and formulation-dependent SKU in the range and needs a brand that already has credibility. Gummies carry a formulation-stability risk (creatine degradation) that must be validated with 6- and 12-month assay data before you put your name on it.

**MVP success criteria — decide at day 90:**

| Metric | Continue | Pivot | Stop |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blended CAC | < £65 | £65–£85 | > £85 |
| AOV | > £42 | £34–£42 | < £34 |
| Bundle mix | > 40% | 25–40% | < 25% |
| Subscription attach | > 15% | 8–15% | < 8% |
| Contribution vs ad spend | > 60% recovery | 40–60% | < 40% |
| Creative win rate (1 in N ads profitable) | 1 in 12 | 1 in 25 | worse |

**`[ANALYSIS]` Pre-commit to these numbers now, in writing, before you have any emotional investment.** The most expensive failure mode in this business is a founder who keeps funding a £90 CAC for eleven months because "the brand is building".

## Phase 2 — months 4–9, expand to 10–12 SKUs

Trigger: MVP hits "Continue" on at least four of the six criteria.

| Added SKU | Rationale |
|---|---|
| **Greens powder 30 servings** | The AOV anchor — £29 GP per unit, highest in the range |
| **Creatine gummies 60ct** | Only after stability/assay data is verified |
| **Ashwagandha KSM-66 90 caps** | Highest-demand herbal; branded extract = differentiation |
| **L-Theanine 90 caps** | Bundle component; enables Sleep & Recovery Stack |
| **Vitamin D3 + K2** | Enables Healthy Ageing and Everyday stacks; seasonal Q4 winner |
| **Omega-3 (or algal)** | Completes Healthy Ageing Stack |
| **Psyllium / fibre** | Enables the GLP-1 Companion Stack |
| **Creatine 250g + electrolytes 15ct** | Trial sizes, already needed for the Starter Bundle |
| **Flavour extensions (electrolytes ×2)** | Cheapest possible reason for an existing customer to reorder |

Bundles live from Phase 2: Foundation, Healthy Ageing, Sleep & Recovery, GLP-1 Companion, Starter.

## Phase 3 — months 10–24, full range (15–20 SKUs max)

Add: Marine collagen, Hair/Skin/Nails, Probiotic, Prebiotic, Iron bisglycinate, Multivitamin, Beetroot, Glycine, plus women's-specific line (myo-inositol) **only once compliance capability is in place**.

**`[ANALYSIS]` Hard ceiling: 20 SKUs.** Beyond that you become the "generic store of random vitamin bottles" the brief explicitly ruled out, and every incremental SKU dilutes the positioning while adding working capital, compliance overhead and dead stock risk. **Range discipline is a strategy, not a limitation** — Huel reached £254m and AG1 reached $600m on far fewer SKUs than most people expect.

## Channel launch sequence

| Phase | Channels |
|---|---|
| **MVP** | Shopify store + **TikTok organic** + **TikTok Shop** + Meta ads (small, testing) + email |
| **Phase 2** | Add Instagram ads, Facebook ads at scale, SMS, affiliate/creator programme, subscription |
| **Phase 3** | Add Amazon UK (defensively — see below), Google Shopping/branded search, YouTube Shorts, wholesale/independent retail |

**`[ANALYSIS]` On Amazon:** list defensively at Phase 3, not before. Someone will list a competing or counterfeit version of your product against your brand name, and you want to own that listing plus Brand Registry. But Amazon is a **margin sink** (~15% referral + FBA fees) and an **LTV thief** — the customer becomes Amazon's, not yours, which destroys the subscription mechanics the whole model depends on. Use it for brand defence and incremental volume, never as a growth strategy.

# PHASE 17 — The faceless content engine

## Target: 5–10 short-form videos per day, sustainably

`[ANALYSIS]` The realistic constraint is not production — AI has made production nearly free — it is **concept quality**. Ten mediocre videos beat zero videos and lose to two good ones. The engine below is designed to produce **volume with a quality floor**.

## The production system

| Stage | Tooling | Human involvement | Time per video |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Hook generation** | LLM generates 40 hook variants/week against a house style guide + the compliance blacklist | Human selects 10 | ~2 min |
| **2. Script** | LLM writes 15–25s scripts from the selected hook using a locked template | **Human compliance check — mandatory, never skipped** | ~3 min |
| **3. Voiceover** | Real recorded human voice for claim-bearing content; AI TTS acceptable for text-over-video only | Founder records in batches of 20 | ~1 min |
| **4. B-roll** | In-house library (product, hands, kitchen, gym, water) shot in batches + licensed stock + AI-generated abstract/mechanism footage | — | ~2 min |
| **5. Edit** | CapCut/Premiere templates; 3 house templates only | Assembly | ~5 min |
| **6. Captions** | Auto-caption + brand style preset | — | ~1 min |
| **7. Variants** | Same script × 3 hooks × 2 openings = 6 assets from 1 concept | — | ~4 min |
| **8. QA + compliance sign-off** | Blacklist check against `04-marketing-and-compliance.md` | **Human, mandatory** | ~2 min |

**`[DERIVED]`** ~20 minutes per finished asset including variants → **6 assets/hour → 8 assets/day in ~1.5 focused hours.** This is genuinely sustainable for one person or one £250–£400/month contractor.

**Batch discipline:** shoot b-roll one day a month (4 hours produces ~200 usable clips). Record voiceover one day a week. Edit daily. Never shoot ad hoc.

## What AI should and should not do

| Use AI for | Do NOT use AI for |
|---|---|
| Hook ideation and variant generation | Any factual or health claim without human verification |
| Script drafting from templates | Final compliance sign-off |
| Captions, subtitles, translation | Voiceover on claim-bearing creative (detectability hurts trust in this category) |
| B-roll and abstract/mechanism visuals | Fabricated testimonials or synthetic people presented as customers — **this is a legal and ASA problem, not a stylistic one** |
| Product visualisation and packaging renders | Before/after imagery of any kind |
| Editing automation, resizing, A/B variant assembly | Deciding what is compliant |
| Performance analysis and hook-pattern clustering | Replying to health questions in comments |

## A/B testing protocol

`[ANALYSIS]` Test **one variable at a time**, in this priority order — the earlier items have 10× the leverage of the later ones:

1. **Hook (first 2 seconds)** — 70% of performance variance sits here
2. **Offer** (Starter £34.99 vs Foundation £64.99 vs subscribe-and-save)
3. **Format** (demo vs explainer vs POV vs comparison)
4. **Landing page** (product page vs advertorial vs quiz)
5. Voice, music, pacing, CTA wording

**Rule:** kill an ad at 2× target CPA with no purchase. Scale at 0.6× target CPA. Review daily, decide weekly, never both.

## 30 faceless video concepts

Organised by product. **Every one has been written to avoid disease, condition, treatment, weight-loss, testosterone and guaranteed-outcome claims.** Where a health claim appears, it is register-authorised wording.

### Creatine Monohydrate (1–10)

1. **"The £0.29 habit"** — hands scooping, coins stacking on the counter to show cost per serving vs a £3.50 coffee. Text-over-video. *No health claim at all.*
2. **"Creatine is not a steroid. Here's what it actually is."** — animated ATP diagram, mechanism explainer, factual only.
3. **"Why your gran should probably take creatine"** — over-45s framing, muscle maintenance and physical performance (authorised claim), no disease reference.
4. **"Monohydrate vs HCl vs 'advanced' blends"** — comparison format, £/gram breakdown on screen. Highly shareable, zero claims risk.
5. **"I read the label on 6 supermarket creatines"** — hands turning bottles, highlighting under-dosing and proprietary blends. Comparison/factual.
6. **"5g. That's it. That's the dose."** — 8-second loop, scoop, glass, done. Pure product demo.
7. **"Loading phase? You don't need one."** — myth-busting, cites the standard 3–5g daily protocol.
8. **"What Creapure® actually means"** — trust/provenance content over factory-style b-roll. Differentiation without claims.
9. **"POV: it's day 1 of 90"** — habit/routine content, calendar time-lapse. Behaviour, not outcome.
10. **"Our batch report, unedited"** — screen recording scrolling a real COA. **Highest-trust content available to us and almost nobody does it.**

### Electrolytes (11–17)

11. **"Water isn't enough after that session"** — sweat b-roll, stick pouring into a bottle, dissolution close-up.
12. **"Three flavours. One is clearly the best."** — poll-bait, drives comments and reorders.
13. **"What's actually in an electrolyte stick"** — ingredient-by-ingredient explainer, sodium/potassium/magnesium amounts on screen.
14. **"Sugar-free vs the neon stuff"** — comparison against mainstream sports drinks, factual nutrition panel side by side.
15. **"The 6am routine"** — silent morning routine, three products visible. **AOV content: shows the stack without selling it.**
16. **ASMR dissolution loop** — 6 seconds, no words, no claims, extremely high completion rate.
17. **"Why hikers/nurses/new parents drink these"** — occupation-based POV series. Endlessly repeatable format.

### Magnesium (18–23)

18. **"Magnesium contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue"** — the authorised claim, stated plainly, on screen, as the whole hook. *Legally bulletproof and it converts.*
19. **"Glycinate vs citrate vs oxide"** — the form comparison the audience genuinely doesn't know. Absorption/tolerability framed factually.
20. **"Read the back of your magnesium bottle"** — exposes elemental vs compound weight. Genuinely useful, highly shareable.
21. **"The 9pm shelf"** — evening routine b-roll, no voiceover, text only.
22. **"Why it's in almost every 'sleep' product"** — mechanism explainer. **Never says the product aids sleep.**
23. **"Three things magnesium does NOT do"** — reverse-claim content. Builds enormous trust, carries zero claims risk, and is almost unheard of in this category.

### Bundles, brand and trust (24–30)

24. **"Everything I take, and why"** — full stack walkthrough, hands only. The single highest-AOV creative format.
25. **"£65 vs £180"** — price comparison of our Foundation Stack against buying the equivalent from a premium competitor.
26. **"What we don't put in"** — no proprietary blends, no fillers, no fairy dust. Positioning content.
27. **"How a supplement actually gets made in the UK"** — factory/GMP b-roll, provenance story.
28. **"We got these third-party tested. Here's the report."** — trust content, repeatable per batch forever.
29. **"Five products. That's the whole range."** — anti-choice-overload positioning; directly attacks the 1,000-SKU competitors.
30. **"The stack for people who want to still be training at 60"** — the brand-positioning film. Facebook-first, 40–65 targeting, the least contested audience in the category.

### Concepts deliberately NOT on this list

`[ANALYSIS]` Because they are high-performing and non-compliant: before/after physiques; "cured my brain fog"; "fixed my anxiety"; "natural alternative to sleeping pills"; "boost your testosterone"; "lose weight without dieting"; any customer testimonial claiming a health outcome; any AI-generated fake person presented as a real customer. **Each of these would out-perform the thirty above in the short term and each carries account-termination and ASA/MHRA risk.** That trade is never worth taking in a business whose entire distribution depends on two ad platforms.
