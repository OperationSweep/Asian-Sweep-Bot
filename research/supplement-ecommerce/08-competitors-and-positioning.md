| Greens 30 servings | £18–£25 | £28–£35 | £45–£79 (AG1) | **£44.99** |

**`[ANALYSIS]`** There is a genuine gap between "commodity Amazon price" and "AG1 price". The £25–£45 band is where a credible, evidence-forward, well-designed UK brand can live — **provided it justifies the premium with something specific and verifiable** (branded raw materials, published third-party COAs, clinical doses, no proprietary blends). Premium without proof is just an expensive commodity, and the customer will find the Amazon listing in ten seconds.

## Where the competitive weaknesses actually are

`[ANALYSIS]` Six exploitable gaps, ranked:

1. **Nobody credible owns creatine for the new audience.** The category is growing at 26% `[FACT]` and every major brand's creatine is a commodity add-on to a protein range, marketed to men 18–34. **Creatine for women, for over-45s, for cognitive/healthy-ageing — is an open position.**
2. **No modern UK brand owns GLP-1 companion nutrition.** The demand is documented and new `[FACT]`; the incumbents are too slow and too brand-constrained to move.
3. **Vitabiotics' life-stage model is brilliant and looks thirty years old.** A modern, well-designed, social-native version of life-stage segmentation is an obvious opening.
4. **Myprotein and Bulk have trained their customers to wait for discounts.** A brand that never discounts and instead rewards subscription has a structurally better margin and a cleaner brand.
5. **Almost nobody publishes per-batch COAs prominently.** In a category where the CRN data shows trust drives 71% loyalty `[FACT]`, radical transparency is cheap to implement and hard for a large brand to match quickly.
6. **Holland & Barrett is a retailer competing in a creator-commerce world.** Their digital grew 20% `[FACT]` but they are structurally unable to be a personality-led brand.

## Where you should NOT try to compete

- **On price against Myprotein/Bulk/Amazon** — they have manufacturing scale you cannot match. `[FACT]` Applied Nutrition runs 46.7% GM *with its own factory*; you will not undercut that as a private-label buyer.
- **On range breadth against H&B** — 809 stores and thousands of SKUs.
- **On protein** — the whey input-cost environment (`+108% WPC, +139% WPI` `[FACT]`) means you would be entering the one category where a $2bn+ competitor just lost 380bps of margin.
- **On generic multivitamins** — Tesco/Boots/Amazon own-label sets a price floor you cannot profitably approach.

## The recommendation

### Position: **"Foundational performance nutrition, evidence-first, for adults who intend to be strong at 60."**

**`[ANALYSIS]` Why this and not the alternatives:**

It sits deliberately at the intersection of the two highest-scoring pure positions — **healthy ageing (51)** and **women's performance (50)** — while retaining the commercial energy of performance supplements. Specifically:

- **It is built on our best product.** Creatine is the hero, and creatine's own narrative is *literally* moving from "gym supplement" to "healthy ageing and cognition staple" `[FACT, 2026 trend reporting]`. The brand rides a shift that is already happening rather than arguing for one.
- **It captures the best LTV/CAC audience.** The 40–60 cohort has the highest disposable income, the highest brand loyalty, the lowest churn, and — critically — the **least contested advertising space**, because every competitor is fighting over 18–34 men on TikTok.
- **It is gender-inclusive without being gender-neutral-bland.** "Adults who train" covers both, and the creative can be segmented by campaign (Instagram/TikTok for women 25–45; Facebook for 40–65) without fragmenting the brand.
- **It has genuine claim headroom.** Every product in the Top 5 can be marketed within the GB register under this positioning. A "cognitive enhancement" or "menopause" brand cannot say that.
- **It is defensible.** Evidence-first + published COAs + clinical doses + no proprietary blends is a *behavioural* moat. A big brand can copy the claim in a week and the operating discipline in about two years.
- **It supports a natural range expansion path**: creatine → electrolytes → magnesium → greens → protein → joint/bone → omega-3 → women's-specific. Each step is on-brand.

## Brand architecture implications

- **Range must stay tight.** 5 SKUs at launch, 12–15 at maturity, never 50. Curation *is* the positioning; a long tail actively destroys it.
- **Never discount the product; reward the commitment.** Subscription discount and bundle pricing only. No Black Friday 50% off — that is the Myprotein trap.
- **Publish everything.** Batch COAs, ingredient origin, exact doses, and what each ingredient does *not* do. Radical transparency is the cheapest differentiation available and it converts in a trust category.
- **Own one number.** AG1 owns "75 ingredients". Huel owns "complete nutrition". We should own something equally concrete — e.g. "**Five products. Every dose from the research. Nothing else.**"
