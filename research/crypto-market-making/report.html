Hedged Market-Making Audit

Prepared for Nuru · research date 26 October 2026 · live data 14:39–15:40 UTC (observation) and 15:47–16:48 UTC (validation) · paper simulation only, no orders, no funds moved

The idea in one line: leave a patient buy or sell order on one exchange, and the moment someone fills it, do the opposite trade on a second, busier exchange. If the gap between the two prices is bigger than both fees, you keep the difference.

Labels: VERIFIED checked today against a primary source, live data or source code. INFERENCE my reasoning from that evidence. ILLUSTRATION made-up but realistic numbers that show how something works, never a forecast.

1. Verdict

Not profitable for a small UK or Nigerian account at the fees you can get today. Keep paper-testing only if you can reach much lower fees.

Why, in plain terms. On the exchanges a UK resident can clearly use, the two fees together cost 0.5% to 1.8% of every round trip. The price gaps between big exchanges are usually 0.02% to 0.06%. To make money, your patient order has to sit so far from the market that it only gets filled when the price is crashing through it, and that is exactly when the hedge price has already moved against you.

What two hours of live data showed. Two separate one-hour runs replayed 2,880 setups (six exchanges, four coins, three fill models). At retail fees, not a single order was filled: a quote far enough from the market to cover the fees was never reached. At a hypothetical well-tiered fee level, the realistic model made $0.05 in total on $2,315 of fills in the untouched validation hour, about 0.2 basis points, which is zero for practical purposes. No setup came close to the 30 completed cycles required in advance, so by the rules fixed before testing, this design fails.

Is any setup worth more work? Only one: a well-tiered account with zero or negative maker fees on the quoting exchange and about 0.05% taker on the hedge exchange. Even then, the best pair in the observation hour made about 10 cents on two fills, and the best in the validation hour about 17 cents on two fills. That is noise, not an edge. Spot only, with coins and cash already sitting on both exchanges, is the only setup that is clearly legitimate for you. It needs no leverage and no borrowing.

Three questions only you can answer (they decide which exchanges are in play; I kept researching without them):
  1. Which country are you legally resident in now, and where will you physically be when the bot runs?
  2. Which exchange accounts do you already have fully verified, and under which country was each one opened?
  3. What maker and taker fees do those accounts actually show you (Settings → Fees)?

3. Ways to hedge, compared

Call the exchange where your patient order sits the quote venue, and the busy one where you hedge the hedge venue.

Selling coins you own is not the same as shorting. In the spot setup you only ever sell bitcoin that is already in your account. Nothing is borrowed, so nothing can be liquidated. A short means borrowing coins (margin) or opening a perp, which adds borrowing fees, funding payments and the risk of forced closure.

4. Worked examples, including a failed hedge

profit per completed cycle (your buy fills, you sell on the hedge venue) = hedge_price × qty × (1 − hedge_fee) − quote_price × qty × (1 + maker_fee) break-even: quote_price ≤ hedge_price × (1 − hedge_fee) / (1 + maker_fee) minimum gap you must leave ≈ maker_fee + hedge_fee + hedge slippage + adverse move

ILLUSTRATION BTC at $80,000. Order size $100 (0.00125 BTC). Quote on Kraken (maker 0.40%), hedge on a deep venue at 0.10% taker. You must bid at least 0.50% below the hedge price, so the bid sits around $79,600.

C. Failed hedgeThe hedge order is a limit "fill now or cancel" capped 0.10% below the expected price. The hedge venue's feed freezes and the price falls 0.6% in 2 seconds, so the order misses. At the 2-second timeout the bot sells at whatever is there: $79,300.$99.85$99.03−$0.82

How D is caught. A cancel is never treated as done until the exchange confirms it and the bot has re-read the order's filled quantity from the exchange (private fills stream plus a REST order check). If the exchange's filled quantity is larger than the bot's ledger, the difference is hedged immediately. In the simulator, a quote stays fillable for 150 ms after the cancel is sent, and those fills are counted and hedged; in the validation hour, 20 of the 45 fills at zero margin landed while a cancel was already on its way. This is the normal case, not an edge case.

VERIFIED Measured adverse selection (mid-price move on the hedge venue 5 s after a simulated fill, in your favour if positive): from −5.7 bp to +11 bp across pairs with fills in the observation hour, and from +0.4 bp to +23 bp in the validation hour. With one to fourteen fills per pair, these averages are not reliable yet.

On BTC and ETH the deep venues hold far more than $5,000 within 1 bp of the best price, so hedge depth is not the limit at your sizes; the limit is how rarely the quote venue fills. INFERENCE Longest tolerable hedge delay. At about 2.5% daily volatility, BTC drifts about 0.85 bp per √second; SOL about 1.4 bp. To keep a 2-standard-deviation move inside a 5 bp margin, the hedge must land within about 8 s on BTC and about 3 s on SOL. Adverse selection makes the real limit shorter, so the design caps it at 2 s.

Do the earlier arbitrage concerns apply? Partly. MARKET hedges and missing timeouts apply to V2. The "failed leg never unwound" problem becomes "unhedged fill never hedged", which V2 has in three ways and V1 has after a restart.

Verdict: adapt, do not reuse as is. For paper trading, use this repo's own simulator (below), not Hummingbot's paper connector. For any future live pilot, start from V1's per-fill logic and add a persistent fill ledger, restart reconciliation, an exposure cap, a hedge timeout and fill-or-cancel hedges. A small custom engine on ccxt is a reasonable alternative.

Design (control loop, in order of priority)

  1. Ledger first. Every order, fill and hedge goes into SQLite before and after each action. On start-up, read open orders and fills since the last recorded trade id from every venue, hedge any difference, and only then start quoting.
  2. Price from hedge depth. Bid = depth-walked hedge price for the full size × (1 − hedge fee) ÷ (1 + maker fee) × (1 − margin). Post-only, rounded to the venue's tick and lot size.
  3. Refresh rules. Replace the quote when the target moves more than 1 bp. Pull all quotes if either book is older than 1.5 s, crossed, or more than 0.5% from the other venue.
  4. Hedge rules. Fill-or-cancel limit order capped 10 bp beyond the expected price, sent on every fill, sized to the filled amount. Retry until 2 s; then flatten at market and pause.
  5. Limits. Unhedged exposure never above one order size; inventory per venue within ±50% of start; daily loss limit of 1% of capital, then auto-pause; manual stop that cancels everything and flattens.
  6. Alerts and logs. Alert on every failed hedge, pause, reconnect and ledger mismatch.

7. Paper trading: plan and first results

The simulator (arb/xemm_sim.py, 15 unit tests) replays every public order book four times a second plus every public trade print, with exchange and local timestamps. A price merely touching your order is not a fill, except in the optimistic model:

  • Optimistic: any trade at or through your price, or a touching quote, fills the whole order.
  • Realistic: trades through your price fill up to the trade's size. Trades at your price first use up the queue that was ahead of you when you joined.
  • Conservative: only trades strictly through your price count, and hedges take twice as long.

Quotes take 150 ms to go live and 150 ms to cancel, and stay fillable while the cancel travels. Hedges take 300 ms (2 s in the stress test). Only completed buy-and-hedge cycles count as profit. Books that are stale, crossed or far from the other venue pause quoting, without assuming every difference is an error.

VERIFIED Thresholds were fixed before any data was seen (arb/xemm_prereg.json). To pass, the realistic model at retail fees needs at least 30 completed cycles, positive net profit (and at least break-even in the conservative model), at least 99% of fills hedged, 95% of hedges within 2 s, drawdown under 2% of capital, and positive profit with 2 s hedge delay.

7.2 Staged plan from here

  1. Done: simulator, pre-registered thresholds, one observation hour and one untouched validation hour.
  2. Only if your real fee tier changes the picture: re-run with your actual fees on your eligible exchanges, from a machine in your own region, for 14 days across quiet and volatile sessions. Freeze parameters after day 7 and judge days 8–14 only.
  3. Report every time: net profit, drawdown, fill rate, hedge completion, time unhedged, inventory drift, and the same numbers at +50% fees and +1 s latency. Keep optimistic, realistic and conservative side by side; only realistic and conservative count.
  4. Live pilot requires your separate written approval, trade-only keys with withdrawals disabled, and a $1,000 cap.

8. Proceed, redesign or stop

Proceed to a longer paper test

Only if the realistic and conservative models both pass every pre-registered threshold at your actual fees, on exchanges you are verified for, on two separate periods.

Redesign

If it only works at "low" fees or only in the optimistic model. Options: reach a better fee tier, test same-exchange BTC/USD vs BTC/USDT, or a less-watched pair on an approved local venue.

Stop

If the realistic model fails at both fee levels on every pair. That is what happened in both runs: no fills at all at retail fees, and no pair reached 30 cycles at low fees. By the pre-registered rules, stop this design on these exchanges unless your real fee tier is close to the "low" scenario.

Main uncertainties: your residence and verified accounts; your real fees; two hours is a smoke test, not a distribution; simulated fills can never fully match a real queue; venue failure risk is not in any number above.
