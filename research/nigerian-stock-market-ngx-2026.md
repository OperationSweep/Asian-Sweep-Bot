## 1. The big picture — why Nigeria is on every frontier-investor's radar

The NGX has been, in dollar and local-currency terms, **the best-performing major stock market in the world in the first half of 2026.**

| Metric | Value (H1 2026) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| NGX All-Share Index (ASI) | **229,419.18 pts** at 30 Jun 2026 | Up from 155,613.03 at end-2025 |
| ASI return, H1 2026 | **+47.43%** | On top of +51.19% in full-year 2025 |
| Market capitalisation | **₦147.2 trillion** | Up ~₦47.8trn in six months |
| Global ranking | **#1 of 92 exchanges** (Bloomberg, dollar capital-gains terms) | Overtook South Korea's KOSPI (~67-68% USD YTD) |

This was **verified against multiple independent Nigerian outlets** (Nairametrics, Leadership, Vanguard, Tribune, The Nation, The Whistler, Nigerian Exchange Group's own statements) during the research.

**What is driving it:**
1. **Bank recapitalisation** — the CBN's programme concluded **31 March 2026** with **33 of 36 banks compliant**, injecting **₦4.65 trillion (~$3.38bn)** of fresh capital, ~72.6% from domestic investors. This re-rated the whole banking sector.
2. **FX stabilisation** — the naira has been broadly gaining against the dollar since late 2025; the parallel-market premium collapsed to under 2% and reserves rose toward ~$50bn. A stable/appreciating naira turbo-charges *dollar* returns for foreign investors.
3. **Falling (but still high) rates** — the CBN began cutting, taking the MPR down to the mid-20s%. Cheaper money supports equities, though fixed income still competes hard (see risks).
4. **Strong corporate earnings** — banks, cement, telecoms and oil & gas all beat forecasts in Q1 2026.
5. **The Dangote Refinery IPO** — a ~$39.1bn listing expected around **September 2026** that could equal ~35% of total NGX market cap and trigger frontier-index re-inclusion (FTSE Russell) and large foreign inflows.

CardinalStone Research forecasts a **further ~24.9% return in H2 2026**.

## 4. The risks — read this before you buy anything

1. **Stretched valuations / correction risk.** After +47% in six months (on top of +51% in 2025), the market is not cheap in aggregate. June 2026 already saw a ~9–10% pullback (investors lost ~₦13trn in a month). Analysts explicitly warn to switch from momentum to **fundamentals-driven selection**.
2. **Election-year seasonality.** 2027 is a general-election year. Historically the ASI has been **negative in August–September** of pre-election years, and political uncertainty builds through H2 2026.
3. **Fixed income competes hard.** T-bills yield ~18–21% and OMO bills 20–22% — a "risk-free" alternative that pulls money away from equities. Money-market funds are a genuine rival to dividend stocks.
4. **Dangote Refinery IPO liquidity drain.** The September listing is expected to pull liquidity *out* of existing stocks in the run-up.
5. **Regulatory overhang on banks.** A June 2026 CBN exposure draft would force bank holdcos to hold 20% more capital than operating entities — analysts estimate **>₦370bn** of additional raises, plus a high 45% Cash Reserve Ratio limits lending.
6. **Forbearance tail.** GTCO and Zenith have cleaned up; some banks (e.g. parts of FBN Holdings, 6% cost-of-risk guidance) still carry challenged assets and may not pay full dividends.
7. **Taxes & inflation.** Dividends carry a **10% withholding tax**, and ~15.7% inflation erodes real returns.
8. **Currency risk (for foreign investors).** Much of the world-beating return is a naira-appreciation story — if the naira reverses, dollar returns reverse with it.

## 5. Bottom line — a sensible starting portfolio

For a retail investor who wants exposure to the fundamentals-backed part of this rally (not the speculative froth), a diversified basket would lean:

- **~50% banks** — anchored by **Zenith** and **GTCO** (cheap, high-yield, cleaned-up), with **Access** for growth upside and **UBA/Fidelity** to diversify.
- **~20% energy** — **Seplat** (dividend + production growth) and **Aradel** (buy on dips).
- **~20% industrials/telecoms** — **MTN Nigeria** and **Dangote Cement** / **BUA Cement**.
- **~10% cash/watchlist** — kept dry for the **Dangote Refinery IPO** and any post-election dip.

Buy in tranches rather than all at once (the market is volatile and a correction is plausible), reinvest dividends, and revisit after H1 2026 earnings season (July–September) confirms the numbers.
