# Ops Conversion Studio: Page Recipes

Use these recipes as composable page blueprints. Tailor the proof scenes, copy and evidence to the user’s actual offer. Do not add sections solely to make a page longer.

## 1. Homepage or work overview

Use for an agency, consultancy, studio, SaaS platform or cross-offer business with several connected capabilities.

| Sequence | Section | Required content and treatment |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Global navigation | Wordmark, 4–6 links, rich grouped navigation where useful and one primary CTA. |
| 2 | Hero | Small proof label, display headline with muted emphasis, one-paragraph positioning, primary and secondary actions, 2–3 reassurance points, and a flagship operational UI scene. |
| 3 | Logo/integration rail | Only use real customer, partner or technology marks with permission. Otherwise omit or substitute a non-endorsement capability line. |
| 4 | Service system | 3–5 large linked cards with numbered indexes, a unique mini-artifact and an outcome-led title. |
| 5 | Why this team | Four concise proof cards, each with an original micro-illustration such as release cadence, reply window or business metric. |
| 6 | Customer proof | Real logos, named/approved testimonials or clearly labelled anonymised case-study results. |
| 7 | Industry proof | 3–5 editorial vertical cards with operations-specific artefacts and direct links. |
| 8 | Delivery process | Four-step timeline from discovery to launch and optimisation. |
| 9 | Case-study proof | One strong customer outcome, supporting testimonial and a relevant calendar, dashboard or workflow artefact. |
| 10 | Conversion footer | Direct value statement, primary action, low-commitment alternative and structured footer. |

**Hero guidance:** Use the main proof interface as a composition of one central frame plus a maximum of three peripheral metric/notification cards. It should read as “this is what the product does”, not “here is a decorative mock-up”.

## 2. Service page

Use for a single service, such as web design, product engineering, SEO, growth or automation.

| Sequence | Section | Required content and treatment |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Offer hero | Service index eyebrow, outcome-led display headline, concise scope statement, two actions, two key metrics and an operational-friction statement. |
| 2 | Method proof | “What we design/build/optimise for” heading, original service-specific proof scene, five numbered principles. |
| 3 | Capability pillars | Three high-level cards. Each should describe a working capability, not a deliverable label alone. |
| 4 | Delivery process | Four phases with a time box, concrete activity and deliverable. |
| 5 | Proof strip | Three or four verified outcomes. Use placeholders only when visibly marked. |
| 6 | FAQ | Four to six buyer objections with concise answers. |
| 7 | Cross-navigation | Other services as restrained text-arrows or numbered links. |
| 8 | Conversion footer | Shared conversion band and full footer. |

### Service proof-scene prompts

| Service category | Evidence artefact |
|---|---|
| Web and landing-page design | Conversion wireframe showing hierarchy, CTA and proof areas. |
| Web applications | Operations dashboard with roles, records, queue and status. |
| SEO/performance | Search result layout, Core Web Vitals scorecard or content-priority diagram. |
| SaaS build | Tenant/billing architecture, onboarding path or account-health dashboard. |
| Revenue/growth | Funnel from traffic to lead to revenue, with clearly illustrative metrics if necessary. |

## 3. Industry operating-system page

Use for an industry-specific platform, automation layer, internal system or complex transformation offer. This is the most detailed recipe. It should make a visitor think, “this has been designed for how my business really runs”.

| Sequence | Section | Required content and treatment |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Vertical hero | Precise audience eyebrow, commercial-friction headline, scope statement, two actions, 3 outcome cards and a short problem statement. |
| 2 | First operating moment | An original scene showing the core workflow in action, plus five numbered capabilities. |
| 3 | Second operating moment | A second scene from the customer, operator or finance perspective, plus 4–5 further capabilities. |
| 4 | System library | 3–6 grouped module clusters, each numbered and tagged to communicate build state, integration role or delivery priority. |
| 5 | Trade/practice fit | 5–8 compact cards or labels defining applicable customer profiles, job types or sub-verticals. |
| 6 | Delivery process | Four time-bounded phases including audit, build/connect, training and ongoing improvement. |
| 7 | Proof strip | Four outcome cards related to the operational transformation. |
| 8 | FAQ | 5–6 risk-removal questions. Address existing software, adoption, integration, implementation and ownership. |
| 9 | Cross-navigation | Other industries. |
| 10 | Conversion footer | Shared conversion band and full footer. |

### System-library conventions

Group by customer outcome rather than internal department names alone. Use compact status tags, for example:

- **Core** for the first working layer.
- **Included** for committed scope.
- **Connects** for an external system that remains the source of record.
- **Later phase** for optional expansion.
- **Ongoing** for a service that is not a one-off build.

Do not claim an integration or compliance capability without verification. For regulated domains, include a clear boundary between automation and professional advice, and get sector-specific claims reviewed.

## 4. Industry or capability index

Use as a tight, high-intent directory. Avoid adding duplicate long-form material from the individual pages.

| Sequence | Section | Required content and treatment |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Index hero | Eyebrow, outcome-led display title and a concise scope sentence. |
| 2 | Large linked cards | 3–6 numbered cards. Each has an industry/offer name, one operational tension, a compact original mini-UI, and an evidence or proof marker. |
| 3 | Conversion footer | Shared conversion band and full footer. |

## 5. Newsletter, insights or case-study index

Use when the business needs an editorial distribution surface.

| Sequence | Section | Required content and treatment |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Editorial hero | Eyebrow, display headline with muted emphasis, and 1–2 sentences defining the point of view. |
| 2 | Content grid | Three columns on desktop, two on medium, one on small. Each entire card is a link. |
| 3 | Conversion footer | Shared conversion band and full footer. |

Each content card needs a real and relevant cover image, an accessible alt description, a compact category label, title, short excerpt and text-arrow link treatment. Avoid using a repeated stock-image style.

## 6. Contact page

Use a compact, decisive layout. Do not surround the form with unnecessary sales material.

| Sequence | Section | Required content and treatment |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Contact intro | Human headline, realistic response expectation and optional call invitation. |
| 2 | Form and schedule route | Form on one side, short alternative “book a call” panel on the other. Minimise required fields. |
| 3 | Direct details | Email, phone where appropriate, hours/timezone and location only if real. |
| 4 | Conversion footer | Include only if it does not compete with the page’s form CTA. |

Use labels, helpful field hints, clear error text, a success confirmation and suitable consent text. Do not add a mandatory telephone field unless it is essential to the sales process.

## 7. Legal pages

Use the same navigation and footer treatment but keep the body deliberately quiet. Use a legal eyebrow, clear display heading, readable narrow content column, distinctly styled section headings, date and contact point. Do not turn legal content into a landing page.

## Shared conversion footer recipe

Use the footer band after substantive pages:

1. An orange eyebrow such as “Let’s talk” or an original equivalent.
2. A direct display headline with a softened final phrase.
3. A one- or two-sentence outcome statement.
4. One strong action and one lower-commitment alternative.
5. A structured footer: brand context, grouped destination links, contact route, legal links and social links only when active.

Keep it visually calm. The conversion footer should feel like a natural end to the argument, not a pop-up rebuilt into a section.
